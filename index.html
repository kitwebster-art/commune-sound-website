Our philosophy

At Commune Sound, we see dance as a moving meditation. As we settle into the rhythm, our attention shifts out of our thoughts and into the body. The mind gets quieter and we become more present with the sound, the movement and the people around us.

We create a space where the dancefloor is the focus. There is no choreography and no pressure to perform. We can move hard, slow down, pause and listen, then return when the music draws us back in. We keep the floor free from phones, shoes and conversation so we can stay present with the experience.

We believe we don't need alcohol to reach that feeling. Music and movement have their own natural intensity. As our bodies release endorphins and the energy in the room builds, we feel lighter, clearer and more alive. For us, Commune Sound is about appreciating sound in detail, feeling rhythm move through the body and sharing that energy with others.

FAQ
01

What is Commune Sound?

For two hours, St Brigid's Hall becomes a dancefloor for electronic and acoustic music, free movement and programmed lighting.

02

What kind of music will I hear?

A continuous set that moves between spacious, rhythmic and more driving music. The sound and lighting are shaped live across the night.

03

Can I come alone if I have no dance experience?

Yes. You do not need a partner, any dance experience or a particular way of moving.

04

Is the movement guided, or do I do my own thing?

There is no choreography. You choose how you move, while staying aware of the people around you.

05

What if I want to stop dancing or simply listen?

That is completely fine. You can slow down, pause or listen for a while, then return to the floor if you feel like it.

06

Is Commune Sound alcohol-free?

Yes. Commune Sound is alcohol-free. The room is centred on music, movement and being present.

07

What should I wear and bring?

Wear something comfortable to move in. Bring a water bottle and remember that shoes and belongings stay outside the dancefloor.

08

Why is the dancefloor free from shoes, phones and conversation?

It keeps attention on the music and makes the floor calmer for everyone moving through it. Phones and conversation can stay outside the dancefloor.

09

When should I arrive, and can I enter after it starts?

Doors open at 7:30pm with an ambient soundscape and time to stretch and warm up. The main set begins around 7:45pm. Arrive near the start if you can. Late entry is welcome. Enter quietly and take a moment to settle before joining the floor.

10

Where is St Brigid's Hall, and is the venue accessible?

St Brigid's Hall is at 378 Nicholson Street, Fitzroy North. The venue has step-free entry and accessible toilets. If you have access requirements, email studio@kitwebster.com before booking.
